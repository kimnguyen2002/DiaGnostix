      Blood-pressure calibration

      Calibration status unknown

      Blood pressure is estimated without a cuff from the pulse arrival time. A calibration against a cuff reading makes the estimate personal.

      1. Measure your blood pressure with a cuff monitor while seated.
      2. Enter the systolic (SBP) and diastolic (DBP) values below.
      3. Press Calibrate, then keep your finger on the sensor and the ECG leads attached for 20 s.

      ECG gain calibration

      Gain status unknown

      The risk model reads ECG amplitudes in microvolts, so the device must know the exact gain of its AD8232 board. Until the gain is calibrated, the device uses a fallback model that ignores ECG amplitudes.

      1. Remove the electrodes from your body.
      2. Apply a sine wave of known amplitude (e.g. 1 mV peak-to-peak, 5–20 Hz) between RA (red) and LA (yellow), with RL (green) on the generator ground. A function generator set to 1 V peak-to-peak through a 1000 : 1 resistive divider gives 1 mV.
      3. Enter the amplitude and press Calibrate gain (6 s). If you measured the gain another way, enter it instead.

      Research prototype, not a medical device. DiaGnostix-ECG does not diagnose diabetes. The risk is a screening estimate from models trained on single-lead ECGs and check-up data of Japanese adults (Koga et al., Cardiovascular Diabetology, 2025) and validated on simulated device signals, not yet on recordings from this device. Blood pressure is estimated without a cuff. A fasting glucose or HbA1c test ordered by a doctor is needed for a diagnosis.
